Act 2 · Before they even call

Most scams start with a fake site, a fake pin or a fake ad.

The challenge asks for tools that detect fraudulent links and sites on the open internet, including search engines and Maps. Victims never paste the link into a checker. So RASID goes looking, every night, before anyone clicks.

Chapter 1 · Name it, hunt it

Type a name. RASID imagines every way to fake it.

An officer types "ADCB", "Abu Dhabi Grand Prix" or "Tadbeer", or presses + on a watchlist. RASID generates hundreds of lookalikes and checks which ones are alive.

  • Watchlists for brands, government services, events and recruiters, with official domains and phones.
  • Lookalikes: typos, l↔1↔I, rn→m, Cyrillic letters, "-secure-verify", TLD swaps, subdomain tricks.
  • Lure words in transliteration: naukri, trabaho, joli, velai.
  • Event countdowns: National Day, the Grand Prix, Ramadan, Hajj.

Chapter 2 · Don't judge a domain by its name

RASID opens every lookalike in its own browser and looks.

A similar name alone never flags a site. RASID takes its own screenshot, compares it with the official site, and reads the page like a victim would. Drag the handle on a phishing clone we built for testing.

  • Looks like the official site? Visual match from RASID's own screenshots, plus favicon, layout, text and styles.
  • Malicious links: forms posting to a Telegram bot, shorteners, APK downloads, links to known scam sites.
  • Malicious code: self-decoding scripts, hidden frames, wallet drainers, "no right-click".
  • Payment capture: card, CVV, OTP and Emirates ID fields, IBANs, crypto wallets, "Pay AED 450".
  • Reputation: Google Safe Browsing and VirusTotal, when keys are added.

Plot twist: RASID once flagged appl-e.com, then noticed it forwards to apple.com. Brands buy their own typos. Those now score 0.

Chapter 3 · The fake branch on the map

"ADCB Customer Care 24/7", on a mobile number?

People search Maps for "bank customer service" and call the first pin. RASID checks every listing for a watched brand, emirate by emirate.

  • Phone and website vs. the official registry.
  • Mobile or WhatsApp numbers on bank and telco listings.
  • One number, many masks: the same phone on Aramex, e& and a job post is one actor.

Live: 130 real Aramex and ADCB listings checked through Google's Places API. None were fake. Good news for Aramex.

Chapter 4 · The VIP pass for AED 99

Ads and search results that wear someone else's name.

RASID searches the way a person in the UAE would, in several languages, and follows every ad to its landing page.

  • Meta Ad Library: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads ads using a brand's name.
  • Google Ads Transparency Center for the UAE.
  • Search sweep: "pay fines Abu Dhabi", "دفع المخالفات", "Dubai naukri visa".
  • Paste an ad (text, link or screenshot); Claude reads the screenshot.
  • Cloaking: Googlebot and a UAE phone get different pages? Caught.

Chapter 5 · Scams don't speak English

"Visa free. Just pay the fee." In eleven languages.

Job and visa scams target workers in their own language, often in Roman script. RASID reads public posts and Telegram channels in Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Roman Urdu, Malayalam, Tamil, Sinhala, Tagalog, Bengali and Nepali.

Chapter 6 · Same kit, same crook

Take down the operation, not one website.

RASID joins items that share a favicon, a Telegram bot, an analytics ID, a phone number or hosting, and builds one evidence pack per actor.

  • Kit fingerprints → actors, drawn as constellations on the map.
  • Strike pack: sites, registrars, hosting, screenshots and ready-to-send abuse letters.
  • Case notes in Arabic and English, written by Claude from the evidence.
  • An officer approves every takedown. Nothing is sent automatically.

Real example: job posts in Malayalam, Urdu and Tagalog shared one WhatsApp number. RASID joined them into one actor on its own.
