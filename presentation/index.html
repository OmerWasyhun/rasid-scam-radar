The brief

Find the fraud where people actually look.

The challenge asks for tools that detect fraudulent links and sites on the open internet, including search engines and Maps. Most teams built a link checker. Victims never paste the link: they search, they tap a Maps pin, they trust an ad.

  • Proactive: RASID goes looking; nobody has to report anything first.
  • For the police: a console with evidence and takedown packs.

Chapter 1 · Name it, hunt it

Type a name. RASID imagines every way to fake it.

An officer types "ADCB", "Abu Dhabi Grand Prix" or "Tadbeer" into the command bar, or presses + on a watchlist. RASID generates hundreds of lookalikes and checks which ones are alive.

  • Watchlists for brands, government services, events and recruiters, each with official domains and phones.
  • Lookalike generator: typos, l↔1↔I, rn→m, Cyrillic letters, "-secure-verify", TLD swaps, subdomain tricks.
  • Lure words in transliteration: naukri, trabaho, joli, velai end up in scam domains, so they're in RASID too.
  • Event countdowns for National Day, the Grand Prix, Ramadan and Hajj.

Chapter 2 · Born yesterday

Catch the site on its birthday.

Phishing sites are usually days old when they strike. RASID reads the daily list of newly registered domains and the Certificate Transparency logs, and matches every new name against every watchlist.

  • Newly registered domains: about 70,000 a day, free, no key.
  • Certificate logs (crt.sh): new HTTPS certificates that contain a brand.
  • Smart matching: "tammpay2026" matches TAMM; "trungtammot" (a Vietnamese site) doesn't.
  • DNS, RDAP and hosting: age, registrar, abuse contact, country and network for every live hit.

Chapter 3 · Don't judge a domain by its name

RASID opens every site in its own browser and looks.

A similar name alone can never flag a site. RASID takes its own screenshot, compares it with the official site, and reads the page the way a victim would see it. Drag the handle.

  • Looks like the official site? Visual match from RASID's own screenshots, plus favicon, layout, text and styles.
  • Malicious links: forms that post to a Telegram bot, shorteners, APK downloads, links to known scam sites.
  • Malicious code: self-decoding scripts, hidden frames, wallet drainers, "no right-click" tricks.
  • Payment capture: card, CVV, OTP, Emirates ID fields, IBANs, crypto wallets, "Pay AED 450".
  • Reputation: Google Safe Browsing and VirusTotal, when keys are added.

Plot twist: RASID flagged appl-e.com, then noticed it forwards to apple.com. Brands buy their own typos. RASID now marks those as safe.

Chapter 4 · The fake branch on the map

"ADCB Customer Care 24/7", on a mobile number?

People search Google Maps for "bank customer service" and call whatever pin comes up. RASID checks every listing for a watched brand, emirate by emirate.

  • Phone and website vs. the official registry.
  • Mobile or WhatsApp numbers on bank and telco listings.
  • One number, many masks: the same phone on Aramex, e& and a job post means one actor.
  • UAE ground view: every checked listing pinned across the emirates.

Live test: 130 real Aramex and ADCB listings checked with Google's Places API; none were fake. Good news for Aramex.

Chapter 5 · The VIP pass for AED 99

Ads and search results that wear someone else's name.

The sponsored result above the real one is a favourite trick. RASID searches like a person in the UAE would, in several languages, and follows every ad to its landing page.

  • Meta Ad Library: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads ads using a brand's name from someone else.
  • Google Ads Transparency Center for the UAE.
  • Search sweep: "pay fines Abu Dhabi", "دفع المخالفات", "Dubai naukri visa".
  • Paste an ad, text, link or screenshot; Claude reads the screenshot.
  • Cloaking check: Googlebot and a UAE phone get different pages? Caught.

Chapter 6 · Scams don't speak English

"Visa free. Just pay the fee." In eleven languages.

Job and visa scams target workers in their own language, often in Roman script. RASID reads public posts and Telegram channels and scores them in Arabic, English, Hindi, Urdu, Roman Urdu, Malayalam, Tamil, Sinhala, Tagalog, Bengali and Nepali.

Chapter 7 · Same kit, same crook

Take down the operation, not one website.

Scam sites come in families. RASID joins items that share a favicon, a Telegram bot, an analytics ID, a phone number or hosting, and builds one evidence pack per actor.

  • Kit fingerprints → actors, drawn as constellations on the map.
  • Strike pack: every site, registrar, hosting, screenshots, signals and ready-to-send abuse letters.
  • Case notes in Arabic and English, written by Claude from the evidence.
  • Nothing is sent automatically: an officer approves every takedown.

Real example: three job posts in Malayalam, Urdu and Tagalog used the same WhatsApp number. RASID joined them into one actor without being told.
